# The Chronicles of Corva: Year 5

*The world engine's sandbox (offline). Each year is written by the city's chroniclers, each from their own house: its loyalties, its grudges, its beliefs about the Flight. These are their claims, not facts, and they disagree. What actually happened is the engine's record, in the season logs.*

## As Iola Aurelian, scribe of the sea temple, of House Aurelian wrote it

In the year of the winds' sighing and the waves' murmur, we, the faithful servants of the sea temple, observed much within our borders and beyond. We saw the hunger of some houses grow stronger than others, yet still, the heart of our faith remained steadfast. Lysandra Veyra and Junius Theren united under the vows of marriage, bringing joy to many hearts. Yet, amidst these celebrations, whispers of unrest lingered among the populace. House Corvane sought answers concerning Livia Corvane and Soren of the Harbor, who had vanished without a trace during the searchings of the previous months. Their absence cast shadows over the hopes and fears of those around them. Our house, House Aurelian, stood firm in our belief that Livia Corvane had chosen her path willingly. However, suspicions grew regarding the actions of House Theren, whom we detested deeply. Despite threats and accusations, no concrete evidence emerged to confirm or deny their involvement in the mysterious disappearance of Livia and Soren. During the harvest season, we witnessed the passing of Phaedra Severin, a pillar of strength in our community. Her death brought forth tributes of respect and remembrance. Meanwhile, tensions escalated between Houses Valerius and Solenne, culminating in confrontations both public and private. These conflicts did little to ease the overall tension that permeated the air. As winter approached, the sea temple hosted a solemn ritual dedicated to the memory of our ancestors and the guidance of the divine. This act served as a reminder of our duty and the unwavering commitment to our traditions. Our prayers echoed through the halls, seeking solace and direction amid the uncertainties of the year. Through it all, we continued to uphold the legacy of our founding priests, whose promises led to the construction of the sacred sea temple. It was a testament to the enduring bond between man and the sea, a connection that guided us through trials and triumphs alike.

## As Aldric Severin, eldest of the judges, of House Severin wrote it

In the year of the winds' sighing, we, the Severins, faced trials both within and beyond our walls. Spring brought hunger to many households, yet amidst these hardships, marriages occurred—Xanthos Valerius and Rhea Marcellan united under the vows of matrimony, while Lysandra Theren reached maturity. Our inquiries into House Theren yielded little, though suspicions lingered. Threats flew between Houses Valerius and Corvane, and threats also echoed through House Theren’s name. Summer saw more hunger, and new alliances formed. Children were born, including a son named Xanthos Marcellan to Clio Valerius and Orin Marcellan. Yet, tensions continued to simmer. Decimus Corvane sought answers in the mountains, searching for those who had vanished, but his efforts bore no fruit. Meanwhile, Lucan Valerius attempted to expose secrets, though he met resistance. House Theren remained elusive, evading capture despite repeated searches. Autumn brought further uncertainty. We mourned the passing of Phaedra Severin, aged sixty-seven, a pillar of strength in our household. Decimus Corvane challenged Rufus Cassian publicly, and House Valerius once again targeted House Solenne, though their attempts fell short. House Marcellan spoke out, and Octavia Marcellan ventured into the harbor, seeking clues, but she too returned empty-handed. House Cassian engaged in conflicts, but their endeavors proved inconclusive. Quintus Severin placed guards at the market, vigilant for any signs of trouble. Winter brought another round of hunger, and whispers of the Flight persisted. House Theren was suspected of orchestrating the escape, while others believed Soren of the Harbor had abducted Livia Corvane. Despite these speculations, our trust in House Theren waned, and we grew wary of House Solenne. Dreams painted vivid scenes, some filled with celestial bodies, others with ancient glories, hinting at hidden truths beneath the surface. Our faith in the First Judgment remains steadfast, echoing through generations. It was here, in the halls of justice, that our ancestors laid down the foundation upon which our house stands today. This year, we stood firm, facing challenges with the resolve passed down through the ages.

## As Basil Marcellan, merchant of the counting house on the quay, of House Marcellan wrote it

In the year of the winds' sighing, we House Marcellan saw much movement among the nobles of Corva. We watched closely, noting alliances formed and broken, threats issued and countered, and whispers of secrets passed between families. We noted how House Valerius and House Cassian struggled through the seasons, their fortunes ebbing and flowing like tides. Their hunger seemed to grow more acute, mirroring the discontent within their ranks. Yet amidst these struggles, there were moments of triumph and defeat, revealing the shifting allegiances and loyalties that defined the year. Lysandra Veyra and Junius Theren's marriage marked a significant union, strengthening bonds between our house and House Theren. This alliance, however, did little to ease tensions between us and House Corvane, who continued to threaten and counter-threaten, leaving many wondering whether true peace could ever come to Corva. Our efforts to uncover the truth behind Livia Corvane and Soren of the Harbor's disappearance remained fruitless. Despite searching the mountains and the harbors, we found no sign of them, nor any indication of their whereabouts. This lack of information fueled suspicion and mistrust, particularly towards House Theren, whose actions had been both bold and secretive throughout the year. As the year drew to a close, we observed a shift in power dynamics. House Aurelian, once seen as a potential ally, now appeared to be taking sides, making it clear that they too believed Livia Corvane had chosen freedom over duty. Meanwhile, House Severin's attempts to expose the truth grew bolder, though their success remained elusive. Despite these challenges, we House Marcellan stood firm, maintaining our belief that Livia Corvane had acted freely, guided by her own desires rather than external pressures. Our trust in House Severin increased, while we maintained a cautious stance towards House Theren, whom we suspected of orchestrating the flight out of spite and malice. Through it all, we remembered the founding tale of our house, remembering that the first settlers arrived on two ships, bringing with them the seeds of prosperity and strength. With faith in our traditions and allies, we looked forward to the coming year, ready to face whatever trials might lie ahead.
