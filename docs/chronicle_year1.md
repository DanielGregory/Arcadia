# The Chronicles of Corva: Year 1

*The world engine's sandbox (offline). Each year is written by the city's chroniclers, each from their own house: its loyalties, its grudges, its beliefs about the Flight. These are their claims, not facts, and they disagree. What actually happened is the engine's record, in the season logs.*

## As Iola Aurelian, scribe of the sea temple, of House Aurelian wrote it

In the year of the rising moon, we saw many shadows cast across the waters of Corva. Our house, House Aurelian, stood firm amidst these storms, guided by the ancient wisdom of the sea. We sought allies but also kept wary eyes upon those who might seek to undermine our peace. We heard whispers of threats from Marius Corvane, yet his actions did little to sway our resolve. It seemed he too was preoccupied with internal matters, perhaps sensing the shifting winds around him. Meanwhile, Gaius Valerius’s search for Livia Corvane and Soren of the Harbor remained fruitless, adding to the mystery that shrouded both individuals. Our efforts to understand the flight of Livia and Soren proved inconclusive. While some believed they fled together freely, others suspected foul play. Yet, none of these theories gained much credence among us. We trusted in the divine guidance of the sea, believing that true answers would come when least expected. A significant moment occurred during the autumn months when Elara Corvane and Lysias Marcellan were wed. This union brought joy to our hearts, though it also served as a reminder of the delicate balance required within our society. As winter approached, tensions rose once more. Theon Corvane’s inquiries into House Theren yielded no new information, while House Theren continued to pose challenges through various means. Their latest proposal for a marriage alliance with House Solenne met with mixed reactions, hinting at deeper currents beneath the surface. Despite these trials, we held steadfastly to our traditions and rituals. At the sea temple, we conducted rites that spoke of our faith and connection to the deep. These ceremonies provided solace and direction, even if the outcomes were often uncertain. Throughout the year, we watched carefully for signs of change—both positive and ominous. Dreams played a role in shaping our thoughts, offering glimpses into the unseen realms. For instance, Seraphine Solenne dreamed of two stars, symbolizing hope and potential conflicts ahead. Drusilla Valerius foresaw a time of great upheaval, warning of impending battles between noble houses. In conclusion, the year passed with its share of uncertainties and betrayals, but we remained vigilant and faithful to our duties.

## As Aldric Severin, eldest of the judges, of House Severin wrote it

In the year of our Lord, we find ourselves amidst the shifting winds of suspicion and intrigue within the ancient halls of Corva. Spring brought forth threats and whispers, but little concrete action. We sought out information regarding Livia Corvane and Soren of the Harbor, yet all efforts proved fruitless. Our attempts to uncover the whereabouts of these individuals have yielded no answers, leaving us both frustrated and wary. As summer approached, tensions rose higher. House Corvane engaged in probing inquiries into House Theren, while House Theren retaliated with threats directed towards House Corvane. These exchanges left much to be desired, neither side able to definitively pin down any leads concerning the missing pair. Meanwhile, House Valerius also conducted inquiries, though they too returned empty-handed. It was during this time that we attempted to expose those responsible for keeping silent on the whereabouts of Livia Corvane and Soren of the Harbor. However, our efforts met with limited success, suggesting that whatever secrets they might hold remain tightly guarded. By autumn, the landscape changed slightly. Theon Corvane began making inquiries, albeit with less success than before. This period saw marriages being celebrated—Elara Corvane and Lysias Marcellan united under the vows of love and fidelity. Yet, despite these personal milestones, the search for Livia Corvane and Soren of the Harbor continued to elude us. Winter brought further complications. The marriage negotiations between Galen Theren and Seraphine Solenne concluded in a betrothal, which seemed to bring some measure of stability to the situation. However, the ongoing searches remained unfruitful, leading to increased anxiety among our ranks. Throughout the year, we maintained our vigilance, always ready to confront any threat posed by House Solenne or House Theren. It is clear that the mystery surrounding Livia Corvane and Soren of the Harbor remains unsolved, casting doubt upon the true intentions behind their disappearance. Our belief remains steadfast: House Theren orchestrated the flight to humiliate House Valerius. While others speculate wildly, we stand firm in our conviction based on the evidence available to us. We trust no one fully, for the lessons of the past remind us that alliances can shift like tides, and betrayal lurks around every corner.

## As Basil Marcellan, merchant of the counting house on the quay, of House Marcellan wrote it

In the year of our Lord 1456, we House Marcellan saw much movement among the nobles of Corva. We sought to understand the intentions behind these actions, always keeping an eye on those who might seek to undermine our position. We first noted the threat posed by Marius Corvane towards House Theren, though his efforts met little success. Meanwhile, Rhea Valerius’s inquiries into Marius Corvane yielded no concrete information, leaving us somewhat puzzled but undeterred. Gaius Valerius’s search for Livia Corvane and Soren of the Harbor proved fruitless, adding another layer of mystery to the disappearance of these individuals. His persistence did not go unnoticed, however, as he faced scrutiny from various quarters. Our own searches for Livia Corvane and Soren of the Harbor also bore no fruit, yet they continued to haunt us. This lack of progress led some within our ranks to speculate that perhaps Livia had chosen to leave willingly, while others believed that House Theren had orchestrated her departure to embarrass House Valerius. Despite these uncertainties, we remained vigilant. Our suspicions grew stronger when Rufus Cassian threatened House Theren, leading to further investigations which revealed more questions than answers. As autumn approached, we celebrated the union of Elara Corvane and Lysias Marcellan, strengthening our bonds and providing hope amidst the turmoil. However, the winter months brought renewed tensions as House Theren attempted to negotiate a marriage alliance with House Solenne, and as Rufus Cassian and Soren of the Harbor seemed to take up positions of influence. Throughout the year, we observed the rise of Dion within House Marcellan, indicating internal shifts within our own ranks. These changes underscored the complex web of alliances and rivalries that defined life in Corva during this period. In conclusion, the year 1456 was marked by intrigue and uncertainty, with many threads pulling at the fabric of our society. While we continue to navigate these challenges, we remain steadfast in our belief that Livia Corvane departed freely, guided by her own choices.
